How this works

What you are being asked for, and what each answer is used for downstream.

Where you sit in the pipeline

Every project you see here has already been through a machine screen and a human desk review. You are not the first look — you are the independent one. If a packet reaches you thin, that is a failure upstream and we would rather have it back than have you guess: use Ask a question, which pauses your turnaround clock.

The three things we need

  1. Categories. Mark what this project actually is. We send you our own categorisation and yours is recorded separately — where they differ, that difference is a finding, not an error to reconcile.
  2. A verdict on each axis you were asked for. Feasibility is whether it can be built. Bankability is whether it would be funded on the terms provided. A project can pass one and fail the other, which is the most useful answer you can give us.
  3. A document. A signed verdict needs its report attached. PDF, because the artifact needs to be the thing a lender receives rather than a draft.

Say when you cannot tell

Cannot determine is a real answer and we would rather have it than a confident guess. It tells us our packet was thin, which is something we can fix. A pass we cannot distinguish from a guess is something we cannot.

Conditional means listing the conditions

A conditional verdict with an empty conditions field reads as a yes to whoever is in a hurry, so the console will not let you sign one. Same for a negative verdict with no blocker named — it cannot be worked or appealed.

Signing is append-only

Once signed, the verdict and its documents are part of the record. You can reissue with a reason and the new opinion supersedes the old one, but nothing is deleted or edited in place. That is what makes the opinion worth attaching to a financing.
